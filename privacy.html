ScholarOS Privacy Policy

Last updated: 13 September 2026 · Applies to: ScholarOS browser extension, version 0.10.0 and later

ScholarOS is a browser extension that copies your upcoming assignments off your college's learning management system and into a planner. This page explains what it reads, what it keeps, and the only two situations in which anything leaves your computer.

The short version: there is no ScholarOS server. No account, no sign-up, no analytics, and nowhere for your data to be sent, because nothing was ever built to receive it. Everything ScholarOS knows about you sits in your own browser's storage on your own machine.

1. What ScholarOS reads

ScholarOS requests no access to any website when you install it. The install prompt asks for no websites at all, because at that moment ScholarOS is allowed to read nothing.

It asks for one school at a time. When you pick your school, your browser asks whether ScholarOS may read that school's web address, and only that address. Nothing else on the internet is included: not your email, not your bank, not your search history. Settings lists every school you have granted, and removing one there hands the access back to your browser.

On a school you have added, and only after you have signed in yourself, ScholarOS reads the page you are already looking at and picks out:

  • assignment and quiz titles
  • course names and course codes
  • due dates and times
  • the link back to each assignment on your LMS
  • the type of item, where the page says so (assignment, quiz, discussion)

That is the whole list. ScholarOS reads the assignment listing that is already on your screen. It does not open other pages, follow links, or crawl your course.

2. What ScholarOS never reads or handles

  • Your school password. You sign in yourself, in your own browser, on your school's own site. ScholarOS has no login screen and no field to type a password into.
  • Duo, SSO, or any other multi-factor step. ScholarOS does not bypass, automate, remember, or interact with them in any way. It simply waits until you are already signed in.
  • Cookies and session tokens. The extension does not request the cookies permission, so the browser does not give it access to them.
  • Your browsing history. The extension does not request the history permission.
  • Anything on any other website. Only the schools you added yourself, as described in section 1.
  • Your Google account, unless you connect Calendar. ScholarOS requests no OAuth scope at all until you press Connect in Settings, and the only scope it ever asks for is calendar.events, which lets it create and update calendar events. It has no access to Google Drive, Gmail, your contacts, or your existing calendar entries, and it never asks for any of them. If you never connect Calendar, there is no consent screen because there is nothing to consent to.

3. Where your data is stored

On your device, in your browser's own extension storage. Two places:

StoreWhat is in it
chrome.storage.localThe import review queue, your import-window setting (how many days ahead to scan), your Gemini API key if you have entered one, the schools you have added, and whether you have connected Google Calendar
IndexedDB, database ScholarOS_DBYour saved tasks, reminders, and class folders, including any document links you have pasted, plus the completion timings described below

Completion timings. When you mark an assignment finished, ScholarOS records four numbers: which task it was, which class it belonged to, when it was due, and when you finished it. That is the whole record: no title, no notes, no content. It exists so Smart Plan can notice that you finish one class's work the night before and another's three days early, and schedule accordingly. Marking a task unfinished again, or deleting it, deletes its timing record. "Forget all of it" in Smart Plan erases the lot at once.

Nothing is uploaded, backed up, or synced. If your browser profile syncs across your own devices, that is your browser doing it under your own account, not ScholarOS.

There is no ScholarOS account, no ScholarOS login, and no ScholarOS server holding a copy.

4. What leaves your device

ScholarOS has two outbound network paths, and both go to Google. Both are optional and both are off until you switch them on: the Gemini API, under an API key you supply yourself, and Google Calendar, if you connect it.

Google's Gemini API, under a key you supply

This powers two optional features: Smart Plan, and pasting text or a link to turn it into a task. Both are off until you paste your own free Gemini key into Settings. Without a key, the features tell you so and do nothing. No request is made.

When you do use them, and only at the moment you click the button:

  • Paste-to-task sends the text or link you pasted into the box.
  • Smart Plan sends the unfinished tasks that are actually due soon: title, course name, due date, status, and the internal numeric ID. It also sends the scheduling preferences you set (preferred study time, subjects you marked as weak, and the difficulty and weekly-hours figures you gave each class), the current date and time, and a summary of your completion timings: per class, how far ahead of the deadline you typically finish, roughly what hour of day you work, and how many completions that is based on. Tasks that are finished, undated, or not due for a long time are not sent at all.

Those requests go to Google under your API key, so they are governed by your own agreement with Google and by Google's API terms and privacy policy, not by ScholarOS, which never sees the response on any server of its own. Nothing is sent in the background, on a schedule, or without you starting it.

Google Calendar, if you connect it

This is off until you press Connect in Settings. Nothing about Calendar happens before that.

When you press Connect, two things are asked for and both are shown to you by your browser: permission for ScholarOS to reach Google's API, and a Google sign-in asking only for the calendar.events scope. That scope lets ScholarOS create and update events. It does not let ScholarOS read your existing calendar, your contacts, your email, or anything else in your account.

Once connected, and only when you press the sync button on a Smart Plan, ScholarOS sends the study blocks in that plan: a title, a start and end time, and the name of the class each block is for. Nothing else about your account leaves the device, and nothing syncs on a schedule or in the background.

Pressing Disconnect in Settings clears the connection and tells Google to forget the grant, so it stops showing in your Google account's connected-apps list rather than lingering there invisibly.

About your Gemini API key

Your key is stored in your browser's extension storage, on your device, in plain text. That is the same way any browser extension setting is stored. It is sent to Google, and to nowhere else, as part of the API requests above. You can clear it at any time by emptying the field in Settings. You can also revoke it entirely at Google AI Studio, which takes effect regardless of what ScholarOS does.

5. Document links

ScholarOS can remember which documents belong to which class. A document reaches it as a link you paste in yourself. ScholarOS stores that link with the task or class, on your device, and opens it in a new tab when you click it.

ScholarOS never fetches, downloads, reads, or previews the contents of a linked document. It stores a string of text and opens it in a tab, which is the same thing a bookmark does.

6. Permissions, and why each one exists

PermissionWhy
sidePanelShows the planner in the browser's side panel next to your LMS page
storageSaves your tasks, settings, and review queue on your device (section 3)
identitySigns you in to Google, only if you connect Google Calendar. Requests the calendar.events scope and nothing else
scriptingPoints ScholarOS at a school the moment you add it, instead of at the next browser restart. It is never used on a site you have not added
Access to the school you chooseReads the assignment list off the page you are signed into (section 1). Granted by you, one school at a time, and revocable in Settings

There are no others. In particular there is no activeTab, no cookies, no history, and no OAuth scope beyond calendar.events, which is requested only if you connect Google Calendar. ScholarOS has never had a server, and adding Canvas, Blackboard and Moodle did not change that: those systems are read through your own school's web address, using the session you are already signed into, and nothing is sent anywhere else.

7. Payment

ScholarOS costs $2.99 once, after a 14-day trial, with no account and no subscription. Payment is handled by Stripe on Stripe's own hosted page in your browser. You receive a license key and paste it into Settings, where it is stored locally like any other setting.

Card numbers, billing addresses, and payment details never pass through the extension and are never visible to it. ScholarOS's entire relationship with a paying student is one license key sitting in their own browser storage.

The license key is checked on your own device and is never sent anywhere. Validation is a signature check performed in your browser, so it works with no internet connection and produces no network request. ScholarOS learns nothing about when or whether you use it.

The trial starts the first time you open ScholarOS and needs no card, no email address, and no sign-up. When it ends, the planner keeps working in full and only the paid extras stop.

8. Children

ScholarOS is built for college students and is not directed at children under 13. It collects no personal information from anyone, of any age, on any server. See section 3.

9. Removing everything

Uninstall the extension. Your browser deletes the extension's storage with it, which is every piece of data described in section 3.

There is no account to close, no data-deletion request to file, and no authorization to revoke, because none was ever granted. If you entered a Gemini key and want to be thorough, revoke it at Google AI Studio as well. That is a key Google issued to you, not to ScholarOS.

10. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the date at the top of the page changes with it, and the updated version replaces this one at the same public address. Material changes to what leaves your device will be called out in the extension's release notes.
